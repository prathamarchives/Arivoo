First: translate the mission into invariants
"Everything compounds, nothing disappears" is a vibe until it's two checkable invariants. Every layer gets graded against these:

I1 — Durability: no human-originated fact is ever physically destroyed. "Delete" is itself a new recorded fact (a retraction event), never a removal. This includes wrong AI output — it gets marked, not erased.
I2 — Compounding: every new input must increase the connective value of everything already in the system. Corollary: relationships are first-class objects, not metadata on notes.
The contract that makes both possible: sacred vs. derived
This is the single most important hardening decision, and it cuts across all nine layers:

Sacred data (append-only, immutable, never recomputed): original file bytes, highlights, notes, journal entries, bookmarks, quick-captured thoughts, your confirm/reject decisions on AI-suggested relationships, merge events.
Derived data (pure function of sacred data + pipeline version): extracted text, chunks, embeddings, Kev decisions, auto-discovered concepts, auto relationships.
The rule: any derived row can be deleted and rebuilt from sacred data with zero loss. This is what makes the system future-proof in a real sense — you can swap embedding models, swap Kev for something better, re-chunk everything, migrate vector stores, and your library re-compounds at higher fidelity. Models improve; your corpus doesn't have to be re-created, only re-derived. First, your direct question.

Does reading more form new concepts and compound? Yes — but only through specific mechanisms, and it's engineered, not emergent
Compounding has a concrete mechanics in this design:

Concept saturation dynamics. Early reading mints many concepts. Later reading mostly misses existing concepts in the concept_match cascade, which means its evidence accumulates on them instead of minting duplicates. Marginal reading increasingly thickens the graph rather than widening it. That's the compounding signature: mint rate declines over time while evidence-per-concept and cross-source corroboration rise. This is measurable nightly — your mission statement becomes a report, not a vibe.
Old inputs gain value from new inputs. A provisional concept born from one book graduates when a paper three weeks later independently touches it. A highlight from last month becomes an asserted edge when today's reading corroborates it. Nothing about the old object changed — the new input raised its value. That is the definition of compounding, and the belief system below is the machinery that produces it.
Re-derivation compounds retroactively. Because sacred/derived is enforced, a better embedding model or a better Kev next year re-reads your entire library at higher fidelity. Your corpus appreciates with model progress without you touching it.
Math is a special case worth naming: definitions are explicit there, so structural detection is strongest — but general embeddings handle notation poorly, so the evidence burden shifts toward structural markers and Kev. Math gets its own eval slice and starts at lower autonomy.

Compounding reverses into noise through five failure modes, each of which now has a named counter-mechanism: junk concepts (mint gate), conflation (context-sense clustering + split detection), fragmentation (arrival-order stability + soft-merge detection), stale beliefs (decay + re-evaluation on revision supersession), hub capture (degree caps + hub penalty — already in your doc). Nothing here compounds by default. Every mechanism below exists to make the compounding direction the cheap direction and the noise direction the expensive one.

1. The belief substrate (replaces accepted/rejected as the core edge state)
An edge stops being a decision and becomes a continuously scored belief. This is the object the whole autonomy system stands on, so the math needs to be exact.

Evidence unit. The atom of belief is (triple, source_work_id, content_hash, family, p_cal, observed_at, actor). Independence is defined on (source_work_id, content_hash) — never on row count. Ten chunks of one book and the same quote in two editions are one unit. Families: embedding, lexical, kev_relation, kev_relevance, kev_evidence, graph_context, structural, agent_corroboration.

Aggregation — deterministic pure function:

text

belief(triple, policy_version, as_of):
  if human_asserted(triple):        return 1.0        // authority, never decays
  if human_deleted(triple):         excluded entirely  // absolute (INV-08 preserved)

                                                      // correlated → take max
  P     = 1 - Π_u (1 - w_u · p_u · decay(u, as_of))  // across units → noisy-OR
  return min(P, 0.995)                                // autonomy never equals authority
w_u is the attention weight: your fragments 2.0, touched source evidence 1.0, read-coverage 0.5. Decay is a half-life on last_corroborated_at applied inside the function, from evidence timestamps — never a mutable counter — so belief is a pure function of (evidence_set, policy_version, as_of). That purity is what makes replay, rollback-equality and hash-audit possible.

Bands with hysteresis. Asserted: P ≥ 0.90 and independent units ≥ 2 and no contradicting evidence — usable in retrieval and (see §5, this is a real conflict with your CND-03) capped expansion. Associated: 0.60 ≤ P < 0.90 — ranking and "related" only. Dormant: below, or contradicted — kept, still accumulating. Promote at θ_hi, demote at θ_lo = θ_hi − 0.05, minimum dwell 24h, max 2 transitions per triple per day. Flapping is a bug class; hysteresis plus dwell is the fix, and both are property-tested.

Contradiction asymmetry. When inverse-relation evidence grows (supports vs challenges on the same pair), both directions sink toward dormant and the contradiction is recorded. The system prefers missing an edge over asserting a wrong one, because a wrong assertion costs trust in every other edge and a dormant edge only costs recall.

Negative memory without you. When contradiction crosses threshold, the weaker belief self-retires: dormant with reason contradicted, blocked from re-assertion unless a new evidence unit — one not in the prior evidence set — arrives. This is INV-08's logic with actor = policy@version instead of you. Your deletions remain in a separate, absolute class: nothing resurrects them but your re-assertion.

3. Concept induction — the full mechanism
Seven stages, each with a gate that can fail independently:

Detection (attention-first). Initiating evidence tiers: T1 = direct touch (highlight, note, bookmark, @mention, drag/cite) — initiates, weight 1.0; T2 = reading-position coverage above threshold on a section plus a structural marker — initiates, weight 0.5; T3 = untouched text — corroborates only, can never start a candidate. The graph reflects what you engaged with, not the whole library. Structural markers: glossary entries, index terms, definition patterns (regex prefilter + Kev concept_defined), italic/quoted first use, headings, abbreviation introductions, "such as" lists, math definition/theorem/notation environments.

Clustering by (normalized form, context sense). Normalization merges only orthographic variants (case, NFKC, ligatures, hyphenation) — never sense variants. Sense separation is context-embedding clustering over surrounding sentence + heading path + source domain. "Kernel" in an OS chapter and in a functional-analysis chapter become two clusters by construction. Alias-vs-distinct-concept is always a decided question, never a normalization.

Judgment. New versioned questions: concept_novel.v1 (is this phrase a reusable concept here?), concept_sense.v1 (same sense as this cluster?), concept_defined.v1. Plus the existing concept_match.v1 cascade. Many small noisy votes, independence counted per source and per family — the same rule as edges, which is what makes the aggregate reliable even when individual votes aren't.

Mint gate — all six must hold:

Weighted independent units ≥ 3 (or ≥ 2 with a definition-site at T1)
Distinctiveness: not in the generic-vocabulary stoplist, specificity score above θ (corpus IDF + embedding distance from the generic-word centroid), 1–6 tokens
concept_match returns none or related-distinct against every existing concept and every pending candidate in the batch
Intra-cluster context coherence ≥ θ
concept_mint breaker green, daily budget available
Not better explained as an alias of an existing concept (else it routes to alias_add)
Birth — extractive only. Label = the definition-site surface form, else the most central form by frequency × specificity. Gloss = best defining sentence, verbatim, with provenance. Aliases = variants seen in ≥ 2 units. Embedding = centroid of established evidence. State = provisional. Run ID, policy version, evidence-set hash committed. No generated text anywhere in a birth — this is both a quality rule and a testable invariant (label and gloss must appear verbatim in evidence).

Probation. Graduation requires out-of-sample evidence that arrived after birth — the concept cannot confirm itself — plus stability under held-out re-clustering. Your @mention graduates instantly: human use is the strongest signal that exists in this system. Timeout → dormant, never deleted; a later evidence surge reopens probation. Time-to-birth and probation survival are tracked distributions, not vibes.

Maintenance. Alias-add: new variant ≥ 2 units + same verdict, auto at high confidence (cheap, reversible). Soft merge: high equivalence belief between two concepts → concept_equivalence link, read-time resolution, rate-limited, auto-reverses on contradicting evidence. Hard merge: only after soft-merge probation with green merge canaries — this relaxes your INV-12 deliberately, with the two-step structure as the safety. Split: bimodal context embeddings + divergent gloss evidence → minority sense becomes a new concept with lineage, and because every edge carries its evidence set, re-pointing edges to the correct sense is precise, not heuristic. Auto-split starts in shadow behind its own canary gate. Centroids update from graduated evidence only, with capped movement per update so a weird week can't hijack a concept.

Actor model from P1: every write carries actor ∈ {user, agent:<id>, policy@version, pipeline@version}. The column exists in the schema from day one, before any agent does — retroactive attribution is impossible.

Evidence unit u — deduped by (source_work_id, content_hash);
                 within a unit, take the MAX contribution across families

  p_u    calibrated probability, per family + question version        ∈ (0,1)

  logit(P) = logit(P₀) + clamp( Σ_u contribution(u), −L, +L )

## Blockers

**1. Age decay on evidence is wrong for this product.** A relation between two passages doesn't get less true because you stopped reading that field. As written, decay depends on age alone. A later corroborating unit adds its own contribution but doesn't stop the old one decaying, so every edge you haven't re-corroborated drifts to dormant. That contradicts "compounds" and "nothing disappears", and it makes projection time-dependent, so everything needs continuous recomputation. Put decay on ranking recency. Truth should change only on explicit invalidation: source retracted, revision superseded, model version deprecated.

**2. The independent-unit key leaks.** `(source_work_id, content_hash)` lets one book count ten times, once per chapter restating its thesis. A chunker or parser upgrade changes content hashes, so re-chunking doubles your evidence. Dependence is also ignored. An annotation on a highlight, ten journal entries rephrasing one thought, and agent summaries of your own KB all derive from existing units. The fixes:
- Key on `(work, stable span)`, sentence-level or anchor-based, not chunk hash.
- Cap or sqrt-scale per work.
- Collapse near-duplicate user text within a window.
- Add a `derived_from` relation so dependents share a unit.
- Give agent output weight 0 in belief. By the doc's own lineage rule it's inferred, so `agent 0.5` contradicts it.

**3. The log-odds form is right in shape and wrong in its priors.** `logit(p) − logit(b)` is a likelihood ratio only if `b` is the class prior of the data the calibrator was fit on. That's the canary corpus's composition, which is arbitrary. `P₀` has to be the live candidate-pool base rate. These are two numbers, the doc blurs them, and without prior-shift correction your measured calibration doesn't transfer. Hubs break it too: many weak topical signals sum to "asserted" for a generic concept like "learning", with only the clamp as a brake. Make the prior concept-conditioned (frequency or degree buckets) so generic concepts yield small likelihood ratios.

**4. `MAX` across families on signed values is a bug.** A weak positive from lexical beats a strong negative from Kev. Take the largest magnitude, or fit a small stacker per unit. Tier weights above 1 on a calibrated likelihood ratio also reintroduce the attention-inflates-truth problem the doc says it removed. Set w=1 and move attention into salience and the prior. The roughly 15 parameters (per-family base rates, four tier weights, L, half-life, per-relation priors) can't be fit on about 100 pairs. Fix most of them, regularize the rest, and report parameter uncertainty.

**5. The measurement plan can't certify what the spec claims.**
- **Sample size:** 100 pairs over about 6 relation classes is roughly 15 per class, and a sealed 30% is about 30 pairs. Certifying "precision ≥ 0.95 with lower bound ≥ 0.90" needs about 150 labeled pairs above the threshold, per question. The Wilson lower bound at observed 0.95 is about 0.888 at n=100 and 0.903 at n=150. Binned ECE on 100 pairs is mostly noise, and "an hour, once" is off by an order of magnitude.
- **Circular canaries:** "Recover canaries with ECE ≤ target" is a training-error test if the parameters were fit on those canaries. You need held-out and freshly generated ones.
- **Contaminated gold:** Public gold (Wikipedia redirects, textbook indexes) is likely in the models' pretraining, so it flatters you.
- **Options that respect no review chores:** label about 100 pairs once, oversampling the high-confidence band, and use them to validate an offline judge model from a different family. The judge then produces the larger label set, with a reported agreement interval. Or explicitly downgrade KEV-04/05 to "proxy-certified".

**6. "Pure function" is contradicted by hysteresis and decay.** Hysteresis makes a band depend on the previous band, so `(evidence_set, policy, as_of)` isn't enough. Either record band transitions as ledger events and define projection as a fold over time-ordered evidence, or drop hysteresis. Floating-point `exp/log` also differs across library versions, so "rollback restores the hash exactly" fails unless values are rounded to fixed precision before storing and hashing. Order dependence (hysteresis, probation-after-birth) conflicts with my earlier "shuffle arrival order, get the same concepts" property. Specify which properties hold exactly and which approximately.

## Serious

**7. One edge table breaks the first principle.** User references and inferred edges have different backup, purge and rebuild semantics. In one table, "rebuild derived" is one missing `WHERE edge_class='inferred'` from deleting your truth. Use separate tables and a `UNION ALL` view for traversal. The "sync problem" argument doesn't apply to a view. "Must have evidence rows" also can't be a CHECK. It needs a deferred constraint trigger.

The round's structural gain: one principle settles three disputes
Before the per-blocker fixes, the simplification that fell out of reconciling blockers 2 and 11 with the agent-role question:

Only decisions made on text pairs — or structural facts — are evidence. Anything that merely points at a pair is discovery.

Expansion paths, hub co-occurrence, agent proposals, graph walks: all discovery. They create candidates, never units. Once Kev (or a structural matcher) decides on the actual texts, that decision is evidence like any other, and its independence is computed from (work, span) of the decided texts — never from how the pair was found.

This kills three birds: my lineage rule no longer over-excludes (blocker 11 fixed — the ablation can show gain, because the expansion generator's value is measured in candidate recall, and the decisions it surfaces are full-weight evidence); agent proposals get weight 0 in belief without making agents useless (blocker 2's last fix) — an agent proposal triggers a Kev decision on the pair, and that is the evidence, with normal weight; and "no concept mints on agent evidence alone" becomes trivially true rather than a special rule. Agents contribute through ingestion (new sources → genuinely new units), discovery (surfacing pairs), and annotation — never through assertion. Clean, uniform, and it makes the spec shorter.

Blockers 1–6, settled
1. Decay is removed from belief. Truth changes only on invalidation. A relation between two passages does not get less true because you stopped reading that field — the reviewer is right that my half-life was recsys machinery misapplied, and right that it quietly converts "everything compounds" into "everything decays unless re-touched," which is the opposite product. Recency lives in retrieval ranking, where it already lives. The invalidation events are: source retracted, evidence revision superseded, decision's model version deprecated. Each zeroes the affected units' contributions (they stay in the ledger, marked — nothing disappears). Side benefit: the projection loses its as_of parameter entirely and becomes a pure function of (evidence_set, policy_version) — simpler replay, simpler hashing, simpler audits than what I had.

2. The evidence unit is redefined on stable spans, with dependence tracking. New unit key: (work_id, span_fingerprint) where the span is sentence-level, not chunk-level — chunks are disposable retrieval containers; spans are stable evidence locators, and the fingerprint is a normalized token-window hash that survives chunker and parser upgrades (this is the chunk/re-chunk doubling leak closed at the root). Per-work pooling: s_w = 1/√n_w — one book restating its thesis ten times contributes like ~3 units, not 10. Near-duplicate user text (ten journal rephrasings of one thought) collapses via the same fingerprint machinery. derived_from edges make dependents share units: an annotation derives from its highlight; agent summaries of the KB derive from the KB. Agent belief weight: 0 (see the principle above). Journal entries count as separate works — distinct authorial moments, not chapters of one book — so your own writing still corroborates, which the mission requires.

3. The prior math is now stated exactly. Two numbers, explicitly different: b_f = the positive rate of the corpus the calibrator was fit on (we control canary composition, so b_f is recorded per question version), and P₀ = the live candidate-pool base rate for that triple class. The correction logit(p) − logit(b_f) yields a true likelihood ratio; adding logit(P₀) re-anchors to the live pool — without this, measured calibration does not transfer, which is the reviewer's point and it's correct. P₀ is concept-conditioned: bucketed by the candidate-pool frequency/degree of the target concept, estimated weekly from live pass rates with Laplace smoothing, and snapshotted into the policy version so the projection stays pure. Hubs get low P₀ — that's the hub brake in the prior, with the degree cap as the structural backstop.

4. Within-unit family selection: max magnitude, signed. MAX on unsigned probabilities letting a weak lexical positive beat a strong Kev negative was a bug, full stop. The representative signal per unit is the largest-|LLR| family. Upgrade path: a small fitted stacker per (question, family-combination), fitted on canary volume (which is generated at scale — thousands of pairs — so this has data). Tier weights: all w = 1. Attention determines which evidence exists at all (you only annotate what you engaged with) and drives salience and concept-detection; it does not multiply truth. The parameter count drops accordingly: fitted = b_f per family (canary volume), P₀ per (relation, bucket) (live estimation, CI reported); fixed-by-design = L = 6, rounding, pooling exponent ½. The scarce 100-pair set validates end-to-end; it never fits parameters.

5. Measurement claims are restated honestly, and the judge path is adopted. The spec will say three distinct things instead of one false one: (i) canary/public-gold certification — pipeline precision ≥ 0.95 at n ≥ 300, LB ≥ 0.90, on held-out and freshly generated canaries (never the fit split — the circularity fix), with the honest caveat that public gold is likely in pretraining and flatters, so it certifies structure (aliases, polysemy, boilerplate) more than semantics; (ii) domain spot-check — the one-time ~100 pairs, oversampling the high-confidence band, yields a CI of roughly ±5 points, certifying domain precision at the 0.85–0.88 LB level in aggregate, not per question; (iii) judge-amplified dev labels — a judge model from a different family, validated against the 100 gold pairs with a reported agreement interval, labels the larger dev set used for threshold fitting. Claims language throughout downgrades from "precision ≥ 0.95 certified" to "canary-certified, domain proxy-estimated." And structurally: the asserted band was never resting on classifier precision alone — it requires ≥ 2 independent units plus no contradiction, so per-unit errors must be independent and duplicated to assert a wrong edge. That compound gate is what canary volume can legitimately certify, and the spec will say so.

6. Exact vs. approximate properties are now enumerated, and floats are pinned. Belief scores: exact order-independence (shuffle property holds exactly for scores). ## 1. How closely does it align with the actual goal?

Your goal is not to create an increasingly large database of notes. It is to build a system where reading, research, writing and thought make future reading, research, writing and thought more valuable.

## 3. Three mathematical properties currently contradict the specification

These are the biggest blockers. An implementation agent cannot make the belief policy correct simply by following the written tests.

### Problem A: the same-work pooling rule does not do what its test says

The formula defines:

\\[ s_w=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_w}} \\]

and applies that weight to each evidence unit from the work before summing the evidence.

The intended property, `S3`, says that repeated paraphrases of the same thesis within one work should contribute no more than a single copy of the evidence. But the formula still allows them to accumulate.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt

Pasted text.txt



The document's own hub example demonstrates the contradiction.

### One weak evidence unit

Prior \\(P_0=0.03\\); evidence LLR approximately 0.524.

# P ≈ 0.050

### Ten restatements from the same book

Each receives a weight of \\(1/\sqrt{10}\\), but all ten contributions are still added.

# P ≈ 0.140

This is approximately three times the single-unit probability, even though the test says same-work paraphrases should not increase it beyond the one-copy value.

The reason is simple: multiplying each of ten contributions by \\(1/\sqrt{10}\\) still leaves a total multiplier of \\(\sqrt{10}\\).

Required fix: aggregate evidence within each work before accumulating evidence across independent works. For example, the system could retain the strongest positive and strongest negative evidence per work, then combine work-level contributions. A more sophisticated policy is possible, but it must satisfy the explicit properties without relying on an incorrect per-unit weight.

That is a policy-level correction, not a reason to redesign the whole architecture.

### Problem B: the hub safeguard can still promote a generic relationship

The proposed exact property says that a generic hub must never become asserted from weak topical evidence. Yet the example uses a hub prior of \\(0.03\\), with each weak unit contributing about \\(0.524\\) log-odds.

Ten independent works yield approximately \\(P=0.854\\), as documented. Eleven such works yield approximately \\(P=0.908\\), crossing the asserted threshold of \\(0.90\\).

So the stated “never crosses” property fails under the proposed arithmetic.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



This doesn't mean that accumulating evidence across eleven genuinely independent works is bad. It means the policy hasn't precisely defined the distinction between:

- A generic topical association.
- A specific, meaningful relation supported by evidence.
- A high-degree concept that appears frequently because it is broad.

Required fix: decide which relation classes can accumulate weak evidence into assertion, and which require stronger per-unit evidence or a stricter evidence gate. A hub penalty and degree cap are useful, but they do not mathematically guarantee that weak evidence can never cross a fixed threshold.

Also, a universal statement about any number of weak units is usually too strong. The exact test should specify the conditions and evidence volume under which the protection must hold.

### Problem C: invalidating evidence does not always lower belief

The simulation property `S6` says that adding positive evidence never lowers the score and invalidating evidence never raises it.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



The first statement is sensible when “positive” means a positive contribution and the score isn't subject to another changing parameter.

The second is not generally true. If the system invalidates a negative contribution—evidence that the relationship is wrong or challenged—removing that contribution should raise the score, all else equal.

The correct invariant is that invalidation removes precisely the affected evidence contribution and deterministically recomputes the projection. The resulting score may rise or fall based on the sign and role of that evidence.

The point of the evidence ledger is to make that change inspectable, not to force scores to move in only one direction.

These three contradictions need to be resolved in the belief simulation before the policy is frozen.

## 4. The probability calculation needs an explicit calibration contract

The proposed log-likelihood-ratio formulation is a reasonable starting point:

\\[ \operatorname{logit}(P) = \operatorname{logit}(P_0) + \sum_u \operatorname{LLR}(u) \\]

Under suitable calibration and independence assumptions, log-likelihood ratios can be accumulated this way. But the formula does not make its output a calibrated probability on its own.

There are two issues.

First, the family-selection rule can exaggerate evidence. Choosing the family with the largest absolute log-likelihood ratio means selecting the most extreme result from several signals. Even if each signal is calibrated individually, that selection procedure can introduce overconfidence. The revised document correctly leaves a fitted stacker as an option, but the final decision needs to be measured on held-out data, not decided by intuition.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



Second, the live prior needs a defensible estimate. The document proposes estimating \\(P_0\\) by relation and target-concept degree bucket. But a model's pass rate is not the same as the actual positive rate. Without labels or a defensible independent estimation method, you cannot infer the true positive rate simply from how frequently the model accepts candidates.

Your no-review-queue constraint is compatible with a conservative, autonomous policy. It just means the system must distinguish:

- Measured performance on canaries and held-out public data.
- Estimated performance on your own material.
- A model score used for an automatic decision.
- A genuinely calibrated probability of correctness.

The revised spec makes a useful step by acknowledging that public-gold tests certify particular pipeline behaviors better than they certify all semantics on your private corpus. It also keeps a one-time target-domain spot check separate from recurring review.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



I would keep that honesty. Until calibration is demonstrated, call the value a belief score or calibrated estimate, not an unconditional guarantee that an edge is correct.

## 5. The evidence-independence model needs one more refinement

The new unit key `(work_id, span_fingerprint)` is a better direction than counting database rows. It helps prevent duplicate passages, different chunk sizes and repeated text from inflating evidence.

The `derived_from` relationship also gives the system a way to identify dependent material.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



But distinct works are not automatically independent sources. A paper can repeat a textbook. A journal entry can paraphrase a passage. Several articles can reproduce the same claim. A PDF and EPUB can contain the same work.

The system needs to distinguish at least:

- Separate objects.
- Separate source works.
- Separate spans.
- Independent evidence lineages.

The last category is the important one for belief accumulation.

Your revised `work` entity helps unify editions and formats. The provenance graph can then record known copying, derivation or source dependencies. Where dependency is uncertain, the system should not claim full independence merely because the work IDs differ.

There is also a semantic distinction to protect: a journal entry is strong evidence that you are thinking about an idea. It is not automatically independent evidence that a factual claim about the world is correct.

That suggests different evidence rules for different relationship types. Your own writing should have high relevance for discovering your interests, hypotheses and conceptual connections, without automatically receiving the same epistemic meaning as an independent source supporting a factual proposition.

This does not weaken your compounding goal. It makes the graph more useful because it can distinguish what you think, what your sources say, and what evidence supports a claim.

## 6. Does automatic concept formation actually work in this design?

The architecture now has the right basic mechanism:

1. Detect candidate concepts from material you engage with and from relevant structural cues.
2. Normalize terms without collapsing different meanings.
3. Retrieve existing concepts and compare senses.
4. Create a provisional concept when no candidate adequately matches and the evidence gate passes.
5. Accumulate new evidence, aliases and relationships, then promote, split, redirect or retire records under versioned rules.

The revised spec also allows retro-scanning the existing library for corroborating evidence, rather than making new concepts wait exclusively for future reading.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



So, yes: the intended system can form new concepts and make the knowledge base compound. There is no requirement that every new idea be filed manually.

But the design deliberately makes concept birth attention-sensitive. Highlighting, annotating, bookmarking and journaling help the system identify what matters to you. Structural markers and reading-position evidence can also initiate candidates under the defined rules. Untouched content can still be indexed and retrieved, but it need not automatically generate a permanent concept for every phrase.

That is a sensible balance between modeling your learning and turning an entire imported library into a graph full of generic terms.

One implementation issue deserves special priority: your revised spec moves the PDF mathematics-extraction spike ahead of chunking and embedding. Keep that. If a mathematical expression or definition is extracted incorrectly, all later indexing and relationship decisions inherit the error.&#x20;

The goal, one sentence
A reading and study environment where nothing you do is lost, every new inputmakes old inputs more useful, and your agents can use all of it.

1. The question, answered straight
You keep reading. What happens, mechanically:

Five compounding mechanisms, in order of certainty:

Deterministic edges compound instantly, guaranteed. Every @mention, quote, drag, bookmark is a permanent edge the moment you save. Your journal tonight referencing last Tuesday's highlight is compounding. This starts at E13 and cannot fail — no model involved.
Belief thickening is structurally guaranteed. Every new source that touches an existing relation appends an evidence unit to an immutable ledger. A paper three weeks from now re-encounters a pair your old highlight started — the old object gained value from the new input without changing at all. The ledger growth is guaranteed; band promotion is policy-gated, by design.
Concept birth is specified and simulated, but its rate is empirical. Attention + structural markers detect candidates; the strict gate refuses junk; retro-scan mines the library you already imported; probation graduates on out-of-sample evidence or your @mention. BEL-SIM and the CNC suite prove the mechanism is sound. Whether it's productive on your corpus at useful quality depends on three measured numbers — extraction quality on your actual papers (E8), candidate recall (E17), Kev's domain precision at threshold (E18–E20). All three are instrumented precisely so a bad number is actionable, not fatal.
Retroactive re-derivation compounds with zero new reading, fully guaranteed. A better embedding model or decision model ships next year → re-derive the entire library at higher fidelity. Your year-old notes appreciate while you sleep. This is the one compounding form that is pure architecture — the sacred/derived split is what makes it free.
The agent loop compounds your reasoning. Agents ingest new sources (new units), surface candidate pairs (discovery), and leave permanent attributed annotations. Your reasoning engine feeds the KB without ever holding epistemic authority over it.

2. Fact/concept, engineered onto what exists
Your epistemology — facts are verifiable objective statements; concepts are abstract ideas that categorize and interpret facts — maps onto the substrate almost without new machinery, which is the sign it belongs:

A fact is a fragment, not an entity. A fact-tagged highlight/note is a statement with provenance (who said it, where). Concepts are entities. The "concept interprets fact" relation is what our typed edges already do (explains, exemplifies, mentions). No new node type — the wiki emerges from what's there.

Two namespaces, deliberately. typed: closed, versioned vocabulary — v1 = {fact, concept}, growing by doc slice later, aligned with the role.v1 labels (claim, definition, example, question) the indexing layer already produces. Typed tags are training signal. free: your strings, organization only, zero semantics. This split is what makes "system trains on it when it is good enough" real without a review queue: tagging is capture-side, optional, zero-friction — it's the labels-from-normal-work mechanism from round 2, now with your own taxonomy. And your accumulated tags become a free gold set: predicting your fact/concept tags is a measurable task — the CNC suite gains tag-prediction precision/recall as a standing eval. You wanted the system to learn from your behavior; this is the cheapest possible form.
One honesty note that protects you later: a fact tag classifies the statement type — "this is a fact-shaped claim." It does not mean Arivo certifies it true. Truth-tracking stays where it belongs: in supports/challenges relations between fragments. The personal wikipedia will show facts with their sources and their contestation state, not a truth flag. That distinction is the difference between a learning tool and a belief machine.
The wikipedia page already exists in the design: a concept's context pack — label, gloss, aliases, evidence with provenance, linked fact-tagged fragments citing it — is the wiki page. Rendered in the UI, served to agents. The personal wikipedia isn't a new feature; it's a view over the concept entity.
